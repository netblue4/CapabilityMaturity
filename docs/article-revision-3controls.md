## A. Replace — "The Dual-Lens Approach: Separating Effectiveness from Compliance"

Providing oversight of a regulation as far-reaching as DORA needs a **dual lens**,
because two different audiences ask two different questions:

1. **Compliance —** can we prove, article by article, that every DORA obligation
   is covered by an owned policy statement and operationalised by a control?
2. **Effectiveness —** are those controls actually working to reduce our
   underlying ICT risks?

We answer them with **three controls that share one traceable spine** — *DORA
article → digital-resilience objective → policy / group-standard statement →
control → risk*:

- **Controls 1 and 2 carry the compliance lens.** Control 1 proves *coverage*
  (every objective is owned by a statement); Control 2 proves *operationalisation*
  (every statement is backed by a control). Together they drive the two gates that
  rate our compliance risk — completeness, then implementation coverage.
- **Control 3 carries the effectiveness lens.** The same controls are mapped to
  the ICT risks they treat and tested through our business-as-usual **Risk and
  Control Self-Assessment (RCSA)** — rated for design and operating effectiveness,
  with residual risk tracked like every other operational control.

Keeping the lenses separate stops us confusing "good IT security" with "regulatory
compliance." If a control fails a test, its **effectiveness** (Control 3) drops and
the underlying ICT risk rises — but our **compliance** picture (Controls 1 & 2)
does not move: the control still exists and still satisfies the DORA objective it
was built for. Regulators get a clean, evidence-backed record of which DORA
objectives are covered and operationalised, without it being muddied by day-to-day
performance metrics.

## B. Replace — §5 "Checkpoint" three-control introduction

We track the compliance effort as its own risk, **"Non-compliance with ICT
regulation (DORA)"** (Appendix 1), and treat it with **three controls**
(Appendix 2) that also give us unbroken traceability from regulation to risk
treatment:

- **Control 1 — Coverage.** Records which DORA articles/RTS apply, the
  digital-resilience objectives we derived from them, and that **every objective is
  covered by an owned policy or group-standard statement**.
- **Control 2 — Operationalisation.** Records that **every policy/group-standard
  statement is operationalised by a control** (with clear ownership) — moving DORA
  from paper policy to operational reality.
- **Control 3 — Effectiveness.** Records how **effective those controls are at
  treating our ICT risks**, through the normal RCSA — the effectiveness lens.

Controls 1 and 2 quantify our compliance (coverage, then implementation coverage);
Control 3 quantifies whether the controls actually work.

## C. Replace — Appendix 2: Controls

### Control 1 — Applicable DORA articles/RTS objectives covered by Policies and Group Standards
- **Objective:** identify the applicable DORA articles/RTS, define their
  digital-resilience objectives, and ensure every objective is covered by an owned
  IT Policy or Group Standard statement.
- **Description / steps:**
  1. Define the applicable DORA articles and RTS — the Statement of Applicability baseline.
  2. Map the DORA articles to our teams and IT capabilities.
  3. Define the digital-resilience objectives to include in our ICT risk policies.
  4. Use the capability mapping (step 2) to identify which policies each objective applies to.
  5. Write the policy / group-standard statements that align the policies with DORA.
  6. Generate the Control 1 evidence.
- **Type:** Preventive, manual (tool-assisted). **Frequency:** Annual + on material regulatory change.
- **Key evidence — aligns to the app's Control 1 page:** the DORA SOA — every
  applicable article/objective and the owned policy/group-standard statement that
  covers it (completeness).

### Control 2 — Policy-statement operationalised by controls
- **Objective:** move DORA from paper-based policy to operational reality by
  ensuring every policy/group-standard statement is operationalised by a control
  (with clear responsibility/accountability), or a disclosed exception.
- **Description / steps:**
  1. Move the effort from paper-based policies to operational reality.
  2. Define the controls — and their RACI (responsible/accountable) ownership — for
     each policy and group-standard statement, linking every statement to the
     control(s) that operationalise it; statements with no control are logged as
     exceptions (E / WT / WP) with rationale, approval and expiry.
  3. Generate the Control 2 evidence.
- **Type:** Preventive, manual. **Frequency:** Annual + on material change.
- **Key evidence — aligns to the app's Control 2 page:** the statement → control
  mapping matrix, with owner and backed / implemented status.

### Control 3 — Control efficacy in treating risk
- **Objective:** confirm the controls that operationalise DORA actually reduce our
  ICT risks — the effectiveness lens.
- **Description / steps:**
  1. Map the operationalising controls to the ICT risks they treat.
  2. Measure their effectiveness — test design and operating effectiveness, rate
     them, and track residual-risk movement.
  3. This ties into our business-as-usual RCSA, where the controls are assessed
     exactly like every other operational control.
- **Type:** Detective, manual (RCSA-integrated). **Frequency:** per the RCSA cycle.
- **Key evidence — aligns to the app's Control 3 page:** controls mapped to risks
  with implementation status, effectiveness rating and residual movement (the RCSA
  effectiveness view).

## D. Small consistency edits (so effectiveness = Control 3 throughout)

**Appendix 1 — Treatment strategy** (replace the line):
> Mitigate the compliance risk via **Controls 1 and 2** (coverage, then
> operationalisation). **Control effectiveness is assured separately by Control 3
> through the normal RCSA cycle** and is tracked against the underlying ICT risks,
> not this compliance risk. Residual gaps are formally disclosed and accepted as
> exceptions.

**Appendix 3 — how it's rated** (replace "proven by Controls 1 / 2 / 3"):
> (1) **Completeness** — every in-scope obligation identified, mapped and
> controlled-or-excepted, proven by **Control 1**. Until this holds, the risk is
> rated High.
> (2) **Implementation coverage** — the band steps down as the *implemented* share
> rises, proven by **Control 2**.
> **Control 3 (effectiveness) does not move this rating** — it is the separate
> effectiveness lens, tracked against the underlying ICT risks.
